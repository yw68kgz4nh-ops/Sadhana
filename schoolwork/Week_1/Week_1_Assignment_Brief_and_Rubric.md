# Week 1 Assignment Brief and Marking Rubric

**Status:** 🟠 Draft assessment tool; should be piloted and refined.  
**Deliverable:** One-page problem and AI-fit brief.

## Task

Choose a real problem in a digital product or service. Describe the person and goal, state the problem without assuming the solution, compare a non-AI option with an AI-assisted option, and identify realistic failure cases and recovery actions.

## Submission checklist

- Person, goal, context, and difficulty are clear.
- Observations and assumptions are labelled separately.
- The problem statement does not prescribe a feature.
- At least one non-AI alternative is considered.
- The case for or against AI is explained.
- At least two AI failure cases are plausible and specific.
- Each failure case includes detection and recovery.
- The next question or test is identified.

## Rubric

Score each criterion from 0 to 3: **0 = missing**, **1 = emerging**, **2 = sound**, **3 = strong and well supported**.

| Criterion | What good work demonstrates |
|---|---|
| User and goal | A specific person, context, and achievable goal. |
| Problem framing | A clear difficulty and impact, without jumping to a feature. |
| Evidence and uncertainty | Observations, assumptions, and unknowns are distinguished. |
| Alternative comparison | A realistic non-AI alternative is considered fairly. |
| AI fit | The case for using or not using AI follows from the task and evidence. |
| Failure and recovery | At least two realistic failures and usable recovery paths. |
| Next investigation | A focused next test or question is proposed. |
| Clarity | The brief is understandable and concise. |

**Maximum:** 24 points. The score supports feedback; it is not a judgement of the learner's worth or potential.

## Feedback format

- **Strength:** one specific thing that works.
- **Question:** one assumption or gap to examine.
- **Next improvement:** one practical change.
- **Reassessment:** what evidence would show that the revision is stronger.

## Pilot note

Use the first submission to test whether the task and rubric are understandable. Record confusing criteria and revise the rubric before treating it as a stable assessment instrument.
