# Coursework, Marking, and Continuous Improvement Workflow

**Status:** Agreed working approach; to be refined through use.  
**Purpose:** Build understanding through assessed practice, and improve both the learner's work and the Sadhana workflow that supports it.

This is a learning and quality-control workflow. It is separate from Sadhana's product requirements, although lessons about how we work may inform future product improvements.

## Core cycle

1. **Teach a bounded module.** State the learning objectives, explain key concepts, and examine examples.
2. **Set a practical assignment.** Give a clear brief, deliverable, constraints, and marking rubric. The task should test the stated objectives rather than merely repeat the lesson.
3. **Allow independent work.** The learner produces a design, analysis, report, critique, or other suitable deliverable. Guidance may be available, but assistance used should be transparent where it affects what the work demonstrates.
4. **Mark against the rubric.** Assess each criterion with evidence from the submission. Give a score or grade, explain the reasoning, identify strengths and weaknesses, and cite concrete examples.
5. **Provide actionable critique.** Prioritise improvements and show what a stronger version could do. Separate essential corrections from optional polish.
6. **Invite response and revision.** The learner can question the mark, explain a choice, or submit a revision. Marking should be open to correction when evidence warrants it.
7. **Review the teaching and assessment.** Ask whether the instructions, lesson, examples, rubric, time allowed, and feedback were fair and effective. Identify gaps in the assessment itself.
8. **Improve and retest.** Choose a specific change to the teaching process or AI workflow and check whether it improves later work.

## Minimum marking standard

Every assessed submission should receive:
- The assignment brief and learning objectives it was assessed against.
- A criterion-by-criterion rubric and mark, with a clear explanation.
- Evidence-based strengths, not generic praise.
- Specific weaknesses, omissions, or unsupported assumptions.
- Prioritised recommendations and examples of how to improve.
- A short summary of what was demonstrated and what remains uncertain.
- An opportunity to discuss or revise the work where useful.

Marks should not imply greater precision than the rubric supports. If evidence is insufficient, say so. Do not grade effort, fluency, or confidence as a substitute for demonstrated understanding. Do not change the rubric after seeing the submission without clearly explaining why and applying the change fairly.

## The second assessment: assess our own process

After marking the work, conduct a short process review:
- Did the lesson prepare the learner for the task?
- Did the assignment test the intended learning objectives?
- Were instructions and criteria clear before work began?
- Was feedback specific, fair, consistent, and useful?
- Did the AI assume facts, overlook requirements, or claim checks it had not performed?
- Was effort added unnecessarily through repeated questions, confusing navigation, missing context, or silent delays?
- What single change is most likely to improve the next cycle?
- What evidence will tell us whether the change worked?

Record observations separately from hypotheses. Do not automatically attribute every failure to the model itself; causes may include prompt wording, missing context, tool limits, task design, interface design, or the workflow.

## Weekly or module-end review

At the end of a week or a coherent group of lessons, review:
1. The assignment and rubric.
2. The submitted work and mark.
3. The learner's questions, revisions, and remaining gaps.
4. The teaching method and assessment quality.
5. The AI's execution, communication, and verification.
6. One or more proposed improvements, with an owner/next action where known.
7. A follow-up check to see whether the changes helped.

## Records and honest status

Keep the assignment, rubric, feedback, revision, and process review in the schoolwork area. If the review reveals a general product problem, record it separately in the appropriate Sadhana design or issue record and link the two; do not confuse learning notes with product implementation.

Use accurate status labels: **planned**, **assigned**, **submitted**, **marked**, **revised**, and **reviewed**. A lesson being prepared is not the same as the learner completing it. An assignment being marked is not the same as the learner demonstrating mastery. A proposed process change is not an implemented or verified improvement.

## First pilot

For Week 1, choose a small, realistic UX/UI task with clear learning objectives and a simple rubric. Mark it, discuss the evidence behind the feedback, and review the teaching and marking process before deciding what to change. Start small enough that the full cycle can be completed and examined honestly.

## Principles

- Feedback is part of the learning experience, not an optional extra.
- Critique the work, not the learner as a person.
- Challenge assumptions constructively and explain the reasoning.
- Preserve learner agency; marks and recommendations can be questioned.
- Use mistakes as evidence for improvement, not as grounds for blame.
- Improve through specific changes and retesting, not unsupported claims that the system is now better.
