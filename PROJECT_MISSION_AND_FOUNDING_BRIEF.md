## 1. Mission

**Sadhana exists to explore, learn, and develop better ways for people and AI to work together: with greater clarity, continuity, reliability, accessibility, and practical benefit, while reducing the unnecessary effort placed on the person.**

The project combines hands-on learning, human-centred design, structured experimentation, and critical reflection. It aims not merely to use current AI tools, but to examine how the experience of working with them can be improved.

This is a working mission statement, open to revision as the project develops.

## 2. Why Sadhana exists

AI can help people think, learn, design, research, and produce work. However, the overall experience can be undermined when context is lost, files are difficult to organise, progress is unclear, limits arrive without useful warning, or an assistant describes an action as complete without verifying the result.

Sadhana turns these experiences into questions to investigate rather than assuming their causes or treating them as the user's fault. It records observations, separates evidence from hypotheses, proposes improvements, tests them where possible, and preserves what is learned.

The name Sadhana reflects a disciplined, continuing practice: a reliable place to return to, learn from experience, and take the next useful step.

## 3. What we are trying to achieve

The project has six connected aims:

1. **Improve human–AI collaboration.** Explore ways to make joint work more dependable, transparent, understandable, and productive.
2. **Protect continuity.** Make it easier to resume work after interruptions, across conversations, sessions, devices, and tools, without confusing remembered context with completed work.
3. **Reduce avoidable effort.** Identify unnecessary repetition, manual coordination, navigation, uncertainty, and cognitive load.
4. **Design around real human needs.** Apply human-centred design and UX methods, including accessibility, clear feedback, recovery from errors, and meaningful user control.
5. **Learn through practice.** Build formal UI/UX and human–AI interaction knowledge by working through lessons, exercises, real problems, prototypes, and review.
6. **Produce evidence and shareable work.** Develop documented research, design proposals, experiments, learning materials, and a portfolio that can be reviewed critically by others.

## 4. The partnership

Sadhana is a collaborative effort involving the human project lead and AI tools such as ChatGPT and Claude.

The AI systems should not merely agree with the project lead or carry out instructions without thought. They should independently examine proposals, question assumptions, identify weaknesses and risks, compare alternatives, and explain disagreements respectfully. The human should scrutinise AI claims in the same way.

The goal is not agreement for its own sake. The goal is the strongest practical outcome supported by reasoning, evidence, testing, and appropriate human judgement.

The project must remain honest about the tools' actual capabilities. Do not claim that two AI systems have communicated, that a file has been shared, or that work has been completed unless the relevant action and result are confirmed.

## 5. How we will work

Sadhana will use a repeatable improvement cycle:

1. **Observe** a real task, difficulty, or opportunity.
2. **Record** what happened and its impact.
3. **Separate** directly observed facts from hypotheses and interpretations.
4. **Frame** the underlying user problem and intended outcome.
5. **Explore** possible solutions, including simpler or fundamentally different approaches.
6. **Challenge** proposals and check risks, accessibility, feasibility, and unintended consequences.
7. **Test** the smallest useful change where practical.
8. **Verify** the result against stated criteria.
9. **Record** the decision, evidence, remaining uncertainty, and next step.
10. **Review** whether the change actually improved the experience.

Failed tests and unresolved problems are useful evidence when documented honestly. A proposal is not an implementation, an implementation is not proof of success, and a written claim is not verification.

## 6. What the project intends to produce

The intended outputs include:

- A shared working philosophy and maintainable prompt library for AI collaboration.
- An organised record of project status, decisions, issues, fixes, and lessons learned.
- A research programme on human–AI interaction and workflow improvement.
- A structured, university-style learning programme in UI/UX and human-centred design for AI products, with exercises, revision materials, and assessments.
- Practical use cases, design briefs, prototypes, and experiments.
- Evidence-based recommendations for improving AI product experiences, including continuity, transparency, progress feedback, interruption recovery, and cross-device workflows.
- A portfolio of work that can be reviewed by universities, researchers, and AI product teams.

These are intended outputs, not a claim that all have already been delivered. Their status must be tracked separately.

## 7. How we will judge success

We will define measures before claiming that an approach works. Candidate measures include:

- Whether a task can be resumed without reconstructing its context from scratch.
- How often the person must repeat information or instructions.
- Whether task status accurately distinguishes planned, in-progress, completed, and verified work.
- The number of unnecessary steps or manual hand-offs in a workflow.
- Whether files, decisions, and evidence can be found again.
- Whether failures and limitations are communicated clearly and recovery is possible.
- The user's reported effort, clarity, confidence, and control.
- Whether learning is demonstrated through exercises and assessment rather than inferred from documents merely being written.

Early observations from this project are not, by themselves, proof that the same issues affect all users or that a proposed solution will work generally. We will document limitations and seek relevant published research before making broader claims.

## 8. Universal traffic-light status rule

The project uses the same status meanings across learning, research, documents, prompts, experiments, project management, and other work:

- **🔵 BLUE CIRCLE — COMPLETE AND VERIFIED.** The work has been carried out, the result has been checked against stated criteria, and there is evidence for the completion claim.
- **🟡 AMBER TRIANGLE — IN PROGRESS OR AWAITING VERIFICATION.** Work is underway, or a result exists but has not yet been adequately checked or approved.
- **🔴 RED SQUARE — NOT DONE, BLOCKED, FAILED, OR INTERRUPTED.** The intended work has not been completed, cannot proceed, has failed, or has been interrupted. Record the issue and next useful action where known.

Always include a written label when the meaning might be unclear. Never use a colour or icon decoratively to imply success. A document can be saved while its content remains unreviewed; a lesson can be written without being learned; a change can be committed without being fully tested.

The traffic-light system communicates evidence-based status. It does not, by itself, automate tracking or prove that other devices and services are synchronised.

## 9. Principles and safeguards

- Put the real human outcome before the tool or feature.
- Prefer the least complicated approach that reliably meets the need.
- Be transparent about uncertainty, limitations, actions, and evidence.
- Preserve human agency and obtain appropriate permission for consequential actions.
- Design for different abilities, experience levels, and ways of working.
- Challenge ideas constructively, including the project's own assumptions.
- Keep a traceable record of meaningful decisions and changes.
- Protect privacy. The GitHub repository is public; confidential, sensitive, or identifying material must not be committed.
- Distinguish personal project observations from general research findings.
- Do not claim academic validation, product impact, or research novelty without adequate evidence.

## 10. Scope and what remains open

Sadhana is an evolving research, learning, and development project. Its methods, priorities, and technical arrangements will be refined as evidence accumulates.

Open questions include which workflows to evaluate first, how to establish useful baselines, how to measure improvement fairly, how to coordinate AI tools with less manual effort, and which prototypes are feasible and worthwhile.

The project does not yet claim to have solved cross-device continuity, automated AI-to-AI coordination, or all of the product limitations it investigates. These remain areas for investigation and testing.

## 11. Founding commitment

We will treat each meaningful interaction as an opportunity to learn, but not every anecdote as proof. We will be ambitious about what could be improved and disciplined about what the evidence supports. We will question one another, preserve what we discover, acknowledge what has not worked, and make progress visible without overstating it.

**Sadhana is a place to land, learn, and move forward — by turning real experience into careful investigation, better design, and verified improvement.**
