# ChatGPT UI and UX Improvement Research

**Status:** 🟠 Amber — initial research area recorded; evidence and prior observations still need reconciliation.  
**Project:** Sadhana — Human–AI Design Partnership  
**Scope:** Improving the user interface (UI) and user experience (UX) of ChatGPT, while comparing relevant experiences with Claude where useful.

## Purpose

Study how the design of an AI product helps or hinders people as they navigate, communicate, collaborate, recover from errors, and turn conversations into reliable outcomes. The goal is to identify evidence-based improvements that reduce user effort, support accessibility, and make the system's state and capabilities understandable.

This is a dedicated research area within the broader Human–AI Design Partnership. It complements research into model behaviour and collaboration; it does not replace it.

## Initial research themes

These are starting hypotheses drawn from project discussions. They are not yet validated findings or claims about all users.

1. **Navigation and project organisation** — make projects, folders, conversations, and recent work easy to locate; provide a clear view of chats belonging to the selected project.
2. **Cross-device continuity** — support a coherent workflow when moving between phone and computer, including voice controls, settings, context, and unfinished work.
3. **Progress and transparency** — show when work is underway, what stage it has reached, what remains, and whether an action actually succeeded.
4. **Accessibility and input choice** — make voice, dictation, and text workflows dependable and easy to switch between; reduce unnecessary reading, typing, and button presses.
5. **Recovery and continuity of context** — help users resume the correct project or thread, identify the last active task, and recover from interruptions without silently changing context.
6. **Honest capability and completion feedback** — clearly distinguish remembering or discussing a task from actually changing a file, running an action, or verifying an outcome.
7. **Conversation-to-workflow integration** — make it easier to turn discussion into organised, reusable documents and maintain a single authoritative record rather than competing copies.
8. **Limits and interruptions** — communicate usage limits and likely interruptions early enough for users to prioritise work and avoid losing progress.
9. **User agency and proactive assistance** — have the AI suggest useful next steps and respectfully challenge assumptions, while keeping the user informed and in control.
10. **Consistent interaction patterns** — avoid making the same basic capability behave unpredictably across devices or modes; provide clear feedback when a control is unavailable.

## Research method

For each observation, record:
- **Problem / observation:** what happened, in which product, device, and interaction mode.
- **Evidence:** direct user observation, reproducible test, screenshot, transcript, or other source.
- **Impact:** what the user was trying to do and the friction or risk caused.
- **Hypothesis:** why the experience may be failing.
- **Proposed improvement:** the smallest useful change and alternatives considered.
- **Acceptance criteria:** observable conditions that would mean the improvement works.
- **Failure cases:** ways the proposed solution could still fail or create new problems.
- **Evaluation plan:** how to test the change and compare results.
- **Status and links:** connect to the relevant journal entry, use case, decision, experiment, or evidence file.

Separate personal observations from generalisable claims. Where possible, reproduce the issue and test with more than one workflow or user before drawing broad conclusions. ChatGPT and Claude should both critique proposals; agreement is not a substitute for evidence.

## Working principles

- Prioritise intuitive design and reducing user effort.
- Make system state, limitations, and successful execution visible and truthful.
- Preserve user context and make recovery straightforward.
- Support accessibility without making users feel that they are the problem.
- Prefer evidence and measurable acceptance criteria over assumptions.
- Keep working notes distinct from approved findings and finished deliverables.
- Maintain one authoritative record and link to related documents instead of duplicating them.
- Use the Sadhana traffic-light convention: 🔴 Red = something is wrong; 🟠 Amber = in progress; 🔵 Blue = finished; 🟢 Green = checked and added to the project.
