# SA-RESEARCH-001 — Human–AI Working Partnership

**Status:** Exploratory question; not yet a validated research design  
**Started:** 2026-10-19  
**Related records:** SA-UX-001 through SA-UX-005

## Working question

Can a structured human–AI working system reduce the effort people spend managing an AI assistant while improving continuity, reliability, and trust?

This question emerged from early Sadhana project work. It is a starting point for investigation, not a claim of novelty or a proven result.

## Why investigate it?

Early project work exposed practical friction: difficulty maintaining conversation continuity, organising project materials, distinguishing a plan from an executed action, and finding a usable read-aloud workflow. These are initial observations from one collaboration and should not be generalised to other people without further evidence.

## Initial objectives

1. Identify recurring points of friction in human–AI collaboration.
2. Separate observed events from explanations or assumptions about their causes.
3. Design small, testable changes to prompts, records, and working procedures.
4. Evaluate whether changes improve outcomes using defined measures.
5. Compare findings with existing HCI, UX, usability, automation, and human–AI interaction research.

## Candidate measures to refine

- How often the user has to repeat previously supplied instructions.
- Frequency of claims about actions or capabilities that are not subsequently verified.
- Number of user actions needed to complete a workflow.
- Whether relevant decisions and evidence can be found again.
- User-reported clarity, effort, and confidence.

These are candidate measures only. Definitions, baseline periods, data collection, privacy protections, and evaluation criteria still need to be specified before drawing conclusions.

## Method outline

1. Maintain dated, evidence-based problem records.
2. Establish a baseline for a small number of recurring workflows.
3. Change one aspect of the workflow at a time where practical.
4. record outcomes, failures, counterexamples, and unintended effects.
5. Review relevant published research before making claims about novelty or general applicability.

## Limitations and safeguards

- Early observations are from a single project and are not representative research data.
- The project must not infer causation from anecdotes alone.
- The repository is currently public; do not store private conversations, personal sensitive information, credentials, or identifiable participant data here.
- Any study involving other people would require appropriate consent, privacy safeguards, and review of relevant ethical requirements.
- No claim is made yet that Sadhana's methods improve performance.
